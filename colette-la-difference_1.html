La Différence Colette

Read this before your first shift — and again whenever the rush makes you forget what this place really is.

We are not a coffee shop

Anyone can sell a coffee and a croissant. That is not what we do. When someone walks through our door, we hand them twenty minutes in France — the smell of butter, a real « Bonjour ! », a pastry made the way it's made in Paris, not the way it's made fast. The food is the vehicle. The feeling is the product.

Who is sitting at your table

Our guest is not just "a customer." They are someone who knows the difference — maybe they've walked the streets of Paris and want that feeling back, maybe they simply refuse to settle for the average café on every corner. They could go anywhere cheaper. They chose us because we promise something the others can't. Every time we serve them carelessly, we break that promise. Every time we serve them well, we become their place.

Three things you must know by heart

Our coffee is not normal coffee. The coffee we pour is found in five-star hotels and Michelin-starred restaurants — and here, at our counter. When you make a cappuccino, you are serving the same cup that white-gloved waiters serve. Treat it that way: dialed in, beautiful, never rushed into a paper cup like it doesn't matter.

Our ingredients cross an ocean. We import our ingredients from France — because French butter, flour, and chocolate simply taste like France, and nothing else does. When a guest asks why our croissant tastes different, that's the answer, and you should give it with pride.

A French pastry chef stands behind every recipe. Our pastries aren't defrosted from a truck. They come from the hands and standards of a real French pastry chef. You are the last step in that chain — how you handle, plate, and describe each piece either honors that work or wastes it.

"Every pastry has a story that starts in France and ends at your table. Your job is to tell the last chapter well."

What this sounds like at the counter

  • Instead of: "The croissant is $4." Say: "The croissants came out of the oven this morning — we make them with butter we import from France. You'll taste it."
  • Instead of: "It's just coffee." Say: "This is the same coffee served in five-star hotels — we're one of the only cafés around that carries it."
  • Instead of: "That's the tiramisu." Say: "Our chef makes the tiramisu right here in the store — it doesn't leave the building until it's on your plate."

The standard

Because of all this, "good enough" doesn't exist here. The espresso gets tasted before the first guest arrives. The case gets styled like a jewelry window. The names get pronounced correctly. The « Bonjour ! » is real. That is la différence — and it's the reason people will drive past ten coffee shops to reach ours.
